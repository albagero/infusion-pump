# Infusion Pump Biomedical Engineering Presentation

## Part 4 – Slides 31–40

### Slide 31 – Smart Infusion Pumps
- Drug libraries reduce programming errors.
- Dose Error Reduction Systems (DERS).
- Connectivity with Hospital Information Systems.
- Event logs support quality improvement.

**Speaker Notes:** Smart pumps help reduce medication errors but still require regular maintenance and software validation.

---

### Slide 32 – Hospital Maintenance Schedule

| Interval | Tasks |
|---|---|
| Before each use | Visual inspection, self-test, battery status |
| Monthly | Clean exterior, inspect cables and accessories |
| Every 6 months | Functional tests, alarm verification, battery assessment |
| Annually | Calibration, electrical safety testing, full preventive maintenance |

---

### Slide 33 – Preventive Maintenance Checklist

- □ Inspect housing
- □ Inspect AC cord
- □ Verify battery operation
- □ Check keypad and display
- □ Verify flow-rate accuracy
- □ Test occlusion alarm
- □ Test air-in-line alarm
- □ Test door sensor
- □ Verify audible alarm
- □ Perform electrical safety test
- □ Record all results

---

### Slide 34 – Documentation and Asset Management

Maintain:

### Slide 37 – Summary

Key points:
- Infusion pumps deliver fluids accurately.
- Safety depends on proper maintenance and calibration.
- Biomedical engineers ensure reliable operation.
- Preventive maintenance reduces failures and improves patient safety.

---

### Slide 38 – Conclusion

Infusion pumps are critical medical devices. Accurate testing, preventive maintenance, electrical safety verification, calibration, and systematic troubleshooting are essential responsibilities of biomedical engineers to ensure safe and effective patient care.
